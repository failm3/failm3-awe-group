# Evidence Note — Brook and Cove Weekday Pilot Sites and the Independent Review

**Scope of this note:** the BROOK and COVE rows of the Service Operations pilot observation log [S2] and the whole of the Operations Quality independent review note [S3]. Alder is referenced only where the log or the reviewer note itself makes a comparison. Charter framing [S1] is cited only where it is needed to state the measurement window, baseline or weekday limit.

**Source tags:** [S1] = `sources/pilot-charter.txt`, [S2] = `sources/weekly-observations.txt`, [S3] = `sources/reviewer-note.txt`.

---

## 1. Brook and Cove observation totals

| Site | Site-weeks | Requests | Delayed acknowledgements | Supervisor escalations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brook | 4 | 480 | 43 | 4 |
| Cove | 4 | 480 | 37 | 3 |

- Brook recorded 4 site-weeks, 480 requests, 43 delayed acknowledgements and 4 supervisor escalations [S2].
- Cove recorded 4 site-weeks, 480 requests, 37 delayed acknowledgements and 3 supervisor escalations [S2].
- Both sites therefore sit inside a four-week pilot window (2026-01-05 through 2026-01-30) at 120 requests per site-week, consistent with the charter baseline of 120 requests per site-week [S1].

All of these figures are cumulative per-site pilot totals for the observation period; the log reports them per site, not per week [S2]. No per-week breakdown for Brook or Cove appears in the log [S2], so this note does not attempt to trend either site week by week.

## 2. Definition of a "delayed" acknowledgement and how elapsed time is measured

- A delayed acknowledgement is one whose **elapsed acknowledgement time is greater than 15 minutes**, and elapsed time is **measured after routing** [S2].
- The elapsed time is derived from **receipt timestamps**, and the pilot's routed acknowledgement median is 11 minutes against a manual baseline median of 18 minutes [S1].

Two consequences follow directly from the wording and are worth stating explicitly, because they bound every count in this note:

1. The 15-minute threshold is **strictly greater than** 15 minutes [S2]; an acknowledgement at exactly 15 minutes is not counted as delayed under the stated definition.
2. Because the clock starts **after routing** [S2], the delayed counts describe post-routing acknowledgement latency. They are not a measure of total customer wait from first contact, and nothing in the log supports reading them that way [S2].

## 3. Independent reviewer sampling scope and reconciliation

- The control arrangement was **one independent reviewer sampling 60 acknowledgement records per site** [S2].
- 60 records per site across three sites gives **180 sampled records**, and the reviewer states that these **180 sampled records reconcile to the weekly observation totals** [S3].

Interpretation, kept deliberately narrow: the reviewer's reconciliation is an assertion that the sample is consistent with the observation totals [S3]. The note does not disclose the reconciliation method, the tolerance applied, or any mismatch that was found and resolved, and this evidence note does not supply those details on the reviewer's behalf. Two further limits follow from what the sources actually say: the sample is 60 records out of 480 requests at each site [S2], i.e. a sample and not a census; and nothing in either source describes sampling for the Depot trial or for weekend periods [S2][S3].

## 4. The reviewer's relative ranking of Alder

- The reviewer states that **Alder has the smallest delayed-acknowledgement count and the fewest escalations** [S3].

This is a relative ranking across the three pilot sites, and it is consistent with the underlying counts: Alder 31 delayed acknowledgements and 2 escalations, against Brook's 43 and 4 and Cove's 37 and 3 [S2]. The reviewer note asserts the ranking without restating the numbers [S3]; the numbers themselves come from the observation log [S2]. Alder's advantage is therefore established on these two count measures only — the sources do not rank the sites on any other dimension, and no statement here should be read as a general quality or cost judgement about Alder.

## 5. The recommendation and the decision gate

- **Recommendation:** extend Alder routing practices to Brook and Cove **for two weeks** [S3].
- **Decision gate:** **repeat measurement before any wider rollout** [S3].

The recommendation is a proposed two-week extension of Alder's routing practices to Brook and Cove [S3]. It is not a completed action, and it is not a wider rollout. The reviewer's own gate makes the sequencing explicit: measurement must be repeated before any wider rollout is considered [S3]. This note records the recommendation as a proposal that sits ahead of both the two-week extension and the repeat measurement — it does not record any part of it as having happened.

## 6. Reviewer caution on weekend performance and Depot outcomes

- The reviewer cautions that **this pilot cannot establish weekend performance or Depot outcomes** [S3].

This aligns with the log's own coverage and with the charter: the observations cover **weekdays only**, and no weekend or Depot conclusion is supported [S1]; the Depot trial is explicitly excluded from the pilot's site list [S1]. The observation log records no missing weekday records [S2], but absence of missing weekday data says nothing about weekend or Depot behaviour, which were not observed [S1][S3].

## 7. Data-quality statement

The log's data-quality statement reads: **no missing weekday records; the log does not identify individual customers** [S2]. Two things follow. First, the counts above are not depressed by weekday gaps [S2]. Second, because customers are not identified [S2], the counts are per-request incidents and cannot be converted into counts of distinct affected customers — the sources do not support any per-customer rate.

## 8. Limitations of this note

- **Bounded to weekday Brook and Cove.** Every figure here describes weekday pilot observations at Brook and Cove [S2][S1]. No weekend conclusion is drawn, and none is supported [S1][S3].
- **Depot excluded.** No Depot outcome is stated or implied; Depot was outside the pilot and is explicitly excluded [S1][S3].
- **No wider rollout claimed.** The Alder-to-Brook/Cove recommendation is a proposal for a two-week extension [S3], gated on repeat measurement [S3]. Nothing in this note describes a completed or approved wider rollout.
- **Counts, not trends or causes.** The log gives per-site pilot totals only [S2]; no weekly trend, no causal attribution and no explanation for the Brook-versus-Cove difference is offered, because the sources contain none.
- **No comparison against baseline denominators beyond what is stated.** The 18-minute manual median and 11-minute routed median are charter-level pilot figures [S1]; this note does not decompose them by site, since the sources do not.
- **Sample, not census.** The independent review rests on 180 sampled records at 60 per site [S2][S3]; its reconciliation is reported as a conclusion [S3] without a published method.
